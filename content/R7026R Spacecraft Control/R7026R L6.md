**Question:** How do we describe the disturbances (solar pressure, wheel imbalance, gyro noise, …) that act on a spacecraft, so that every one of them becomes "white noise through a known filter", which is exactly the model the Kalman filter needs?
**Builds on:** [[R7026R L1]], [[R7026R L5]] · **Leads to:** [[R7026R L7]]

## Summary
<!-- AFTER: answer the Question in my own words, max 3 sentences -->
Process disturbances $w$ move the state (environment torques, wheel imbalance), while measurement disturbances $n$ only corrupt what we see (star-tracker and gyro noise); signals are either energy signals (die away) or power signals (don't), and the spectrum $\Phi_z(\omega)$ tells how much of each frequency they contain, with $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\Phi_z\,d\omega = R_z$ as the size for both. An impulse and white noise both have a flat spectrum and give the same size measure through a system ($\Phi_y = |G(i\omega)|^2\Phi_u$), so any disturbance can be modeled as white noise through a shaping filter: $1/s$ for a constant/bias, $\frac{\omega_d}{s^2 + \omega_d^2}$ for a sinusoid, $\frac{\omega_r^2}{s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_rs + \omega_r^2}$ for a resonance. Stacking these filters with the plant gives one system $\dot x = Ax + Bu + Nv_1$, $y = Cx + v_2$ driven only by $u$ and white noise, the input to the Kalman filter; and after sampling, frequencies above $\pi/h$ fold back (aliasing), so filter before sampling.

## Key concepts
<!-- One line each: what it is + why it matters -->
- **Process disturbance $w$:** Changes the controlled variable itself, $z = Gu + w$ (solar pressure, gravity gradient, drag, slosh, wheel imbalance).
- **Measurement disturbance $n$:** Only corrupts the measurement, $y = z + n$ (star-tracker noise, gyro noise and bias).
- **Energy signal:** Dies away; finite energy $\int|z|^2dt < \infty$ (e.g. a transient after one thruster pulse).
- **Power signal:** Never dies away nor explodes; infinite energy but finite average power (e.g. a torque that repeats every orbit, band-limited noise).
- **Spectrum $\Phi_z(\omega)$:** How much of each frequency the signal holds (power/energy per frequency).
- **Autocorrelation $r_z(\tau)$:** How similar a signal is to a shifted copy of itself. Its Fourier transform is the spectrum.
- **White noise:** Flat spectrum $\Phi \equiv R$: equal amount of every frequency, uncorrelated between any two time instants, infinite power (an idealization).
- **Intensity $R$ (or $W$):** The height of the flat white-noise spectrum. The "strength" of the noise.
- **Colored noise:** Non-flat spectrum = white noise passed through a filter $G_v$.
- **Shaping filter:** The linear system $G_v$ that turns white noise into the disturbance we want to model.
- **State augmentation:** Adding the shaping filter's states to the plant's state vector, so that only white noise enters.
- **Spectral factorization:** The reverse problem: from a measured spectrum $\Phi_v$, find a stable minimum-phase $G_v$.
- **Aliasing:** After sampling at interval $h$, frequencies above $\pi/h$ fold back and appear as false low frequencies.

### One description for all disturbances ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=5|s. 5]])
![[R7026R L6 s5 four classical disturbances.png]]
*The four classical shapes and their sources ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=5|s. 5]]): an impulse (thruster pulse), a step (solar torque switching on when leaving Earth's shadow), a sinusoid (a torque that repeats every orbit) and white noise (gyro noise).*

What do they have in common? **Each is the output of some linear system driven by an impulse or by white noise**, so one description covers them all.

### Energy and power signals ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=6|s. 6]])
A signal that never dies away (a constant, an endless sinusoid) has **infinite energy**. So we need two kinds of size:
$$
\text{Energy: } \|z\|_2^2 = \int_0^\infty |z(t)|^2\,dt, \qquad \text{Power: } \|z\|_e^2 = \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\int_0^N |z(t)|^2\,dt
$$

| | Energy | Power |
|---|---|---|
| **Energy signal** (vanishes in time) | finite | zero |
| **Power signal** (doesn't vanish, doesn't diverge) | infinite | finite, nonzero |

Plain words: energy = the total area under $|z|^2$; power = the *average* height of $|z|^2$ over a long time.

### The spectrum ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=7|s. 7–10]])
**How much of each frequency does a signal contain?**

**Energy signals:** Fourier transform $Z(i\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty z(t)e^{-i\omega t}dt$, and the spectrum is its squared magnitude:
$$
\Phi_z(\omega) = Z(i\omega)Z^*(i\omega) = |Z(i\omega)|^2
$$

> [!question] Fourier vs Laplace (my question from the lecture)
> **Laplace** (L1) uses $e^{st}$ with $s = \sigma + j\omega$: exponentials that can grow or decay, starting at $t = 0$. Good for transients, stability and transfer functions.
> **Fourier** is the special case $s = j\omega$ (pure sinusoids, $\sigma = 0$), usually over all time. It answers "how much of each frequency is in this signal?", which is exactly what a spectrum needs. For a stable system, $G(j\omega)$ = Laplace evaluated on the imaginary axis = the frequency response.

**Power signals ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=7|s. 7–8]]):** the definition above breaks down (a unit step gives $\Phi = 1/\omega^2 \to \infty$ at $\omega = 0$), exactly for signals that never die away. Fix: average over a long window:
$$
\Phi_z(\omega) = \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N Z_N(i\omega)Z_N^*(i\omega), \qquad Z_N(i\omega) = \int_0^N z(t)e^{-i\omega t}dt
$$
Equivalently, the spectrum is the **Fourier transform of the autocorrelation**:
$$
\Phi_z(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty r_z(\tau)e^{-i\omega\tau}d\tau, \qquad r_z(\tau) = \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\int_0^N z(t)z^T(t-\tau)\,dt
$$

> [!question] Correlation vs convolution (my question from the lecture)
> **Convolution** $(g*u)(t) = \int g(\tau)u(t - \tau)d\tau$: one signal is **flipped** and slid across the other. It describes what a system *does* to an input (L1).
> **Autocorrelation** $r_z(\tau) = \text{avg of } z(t)z(t - \tau)$: the signal is **not flipped**, just shifted and multiplied with itself. It measures "how much does the signal now resemble the signal $\tau$ seconds ago". White noise: no resemblance at all for $\tau \neq 0$.
> "Hermitian" is not the name of correlation. It's a property of the **spectrum**: $\Phi^* = \Phi$ ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=11|s. 11]]).

![[R7026R L6 s7 disturbance torque spectrum.png]]
*The spectrum of a real disturbance torque (illustrative, [[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=7|s. 7]]): each source sits at its own frequency: a constant part at $\omega = 0$, peaks at the orbital rate $\omega_o$ and $2\omega_o$, the wheel at 3000 rpm, and a noise floor.*

**A size measure ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=9|s. 9–10]]):** the integral of the spectrum over all frequencies:
$$
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty \Phi_z(\omega)\,d\omega = R_z
$$
where $R_z = \int_0^\infty zz^Tdt$ (energy signals) or $R_z = \lim\frac1N\int_0^N zz^Tdt$ (power signals, = the **covariance**). **It works for both** (Parseval's theorem). Plain words: add up the "amount" at every frequency and you get the total size.

### Properties of the spectrum ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=11|s. 11]])
- **Self-adjoint (Hermitian):** $\Phi_z^*(\omega) = \Phi_z(\omega)$ (real for a scalar signal).
- **Through a linear system** $y = Gu$:
$$
\Phi_y(\omega) = G(i\omega)\,\Phi_u(\omega)\,G^*(i\omega), \qquad \text{scalar: } \Phi_y(\omega) = |G(i\omega)|^2\,\Phi_u(\omega)
$$
  ← **the most important formula of the lecture.** The system reshapes the spectrum by $|G|^2$.
- **Partitioned signal** $z = [y; u]$: $\Phi_z = \begin{bmatrix} \Phi_{yy} & \Phi_{yu} \\ \Phi_{uy} & \Phi_{uu} \end{bmatrix}$, $\Phi_{yu}$ = cross spectrum. If $y = Gu$: $\Phi_{yu} = G\Phi_u$.
- $u$ and $y$ are **uncorrelated** if $\Phi_{uy} \equiv 0$.

### Base disturbances: impulse and white noise ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=13|s. 13–16]])
A signal with a **constant (flat) spectrum**, $\Phi_z \equiv R$ (scalar: $\equiv 1$):
- **Energy signal:** the impulse $\delta(t)$ has $Z(i\omega) \equiv 1$ ⇒ $\Phi \equiv 1$.
- **Power signal:** white noise has autocorrelation $r_z(\tau) = \delta(\tau)$ ⇒ $\Phi \equiv 1$. Vector-valued: $r_z(\tau) = R\delta(\tau)$, $\Phi_z \equiv R$, $R$ = the **intensity**.

![[R7026R L6 s14 impulse vs white noise.png]]
*$G(s) = 1/(s+2)$. Top: one thruster pulse, the response rings down once (energy 0.25). Bottom: white noise, never stops (power 0.25, $\sigma = 0.5$) ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=14|s. 14]]).*

**Two different measures of two different signals: do they have anything to do with each other? ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=14|s. 14–15]]) Yes, they're the same number:**
$$
\underbrace{\|g\|_2^2 = \int_0^\infty |g(t)|^2dt}_{\text{energy of impulse response}} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty |G(i\omega)|^2d\omega = \underbrace{\|z\|_e^2}_{\text{power of white-noise response}}
$$
Because both inputs have the same flat spectrum, $|G|^2$ shapes them identically. → **Impulses can be modeled as white noise (and vice versa).** Minimizing the energy of the impulse response = minimizing the power of the white-noise response (this is why LQR and Kalman are "the same" problem).

**Worked example ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=16|s. 16]]):** $G(s) = \frac{1}{s + a}$, $a > 0$, white noise of intensity 1.
- Frequency domain: $\Phi_z = \frac{1}{\omega^2 + a^2}$, $R_z = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int\frac{d\omega}{\omega^2 + a^2} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{a} = \frac{1}{2a}$.
- Time domain: $g(t) = e^{-at}$, $\|g\|_2^2 = \int_0^\infty e^{-2at}dt = \frac{1}{2a}$.
- Same number; for $a = 2$ both are 0.25.

> [!question] "The larger $a$, the smaller…?" (my question from the lecture)
> Yes: the output size is $R_z = 1/(2a)$, so **larger $a$ ⇒ smaller output**. Larger $a$ = the pole is further left = the impulse response $e^{-at}$ dies faster (less area) = the filter's bandwidth is wider but its gain $1/a$ is lower, so it lets less of the noise through in total.

### The same model for sensor noise ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=17|s. 17]])
An Earth sensor's noise = white noise of intensity $W = 9\cdot10^{-4}$ deg² s through a first-order filter with unit DC gain and corner frequency $\omega_c$ (a real sensor has finite bandwidth):
$$
G(s) = \frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c} \quad\Rightarrow\quad R_z = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int\frac{W\omega_c^2}{\omega^2 + \omega_c^2}d\omega = \frac{W\omega_c}{2}
$$
$\omega_c = 2$: $\sigma = \sqrt{9\cdot10^{-4}} = 0.030°$; $\omega_c = 20$: $\sigma = \sqrt{9\cdot10^{-3}} = 0.095°$.
![[R7026R L6 s17 filtered sensor noise.png]]

As $\omega_c \to \infty$ the variance **grows without bound**: the filter lets through more and more of a flat spectrum. **White noise itself has infinite power; only filtered noise has a finite variance.**

### White noise made precise ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=18|s. 18]])
$$
E\,v(t) = 0, \qquad E\,v(t)v^T(s) = R\,\delta(t - s), \qquad \Phi_v(\omega) \equiv R
$$
($E$ = expected value = the mean over many repetitions.)
- $t \neq s$: **zero correlation** however close in time → prediction is impossible.
- $t = s$: $E\,vv^T = R\delta(0) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int R\,d\omega = \infty$: **infinite power**.
- **Stationary** (statistics constant in time) and **ergodic** (one long record shows all of them): time average = expectation, so $r_z(\tau) = E\,z(t)z^T(t - \tau)$, $R_z = E\,zz^T$. That's the $E[\cdot]$ of L7.

![[R7026R L6 s18 white noise made precise.png]]
*$R = 1$ through $\omega_c/(s + \omega_c)$ for $\omega_c = 5, 10, 20$ ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=18|s. 18]]). The autocorrelation peak $\sigma^2 = R\omega_c/2$ grows, but its area stays $R$; the spectrum is flat up to $\omega_c$.*

Ideal white noise is neither an energy nor a power signal: it's the limit $\omega_c \to \infty$ of real band-limited noise (like a gyro's). We use it because the math stays simple: **one matrix $R$, $\Phi_y = GRG^*$.**

> [!question] What does the spectrum of white noise mean? (my question from the lecture)
> It's **not** the energy. It's the **power per unit frequency** (power spectral density): how much of the total power sits around each frequency. For white noise it's a flat line at height $R$: every frequency contains the same amount. The total power is the area under it, $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int R\,d\omega = \infty$, which is why ideal white noise is an idealization.

> [!question] White noise through a scalar: amplified or reduced? (my question from the lecture)
> A scalar gain $k$ is a "system" with $G = k$ at every frequency, so $\Phi_y = k^2R$: still **flat = still white**, just with intensity $k^2R$. Amplified if $|k| > 1$, reduced if $|k| < 1$. Any **frequency-dependent** $G$ (anything but a constant) reshapes the spectrum by $|G(i\omega)|^2$ → the output becomes **colored**.

### Disturbance models: shaping filters ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=19|s. 19–22]])
**Colored noise ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=19|s. 19]]):** a non-flat spectrum = white noise $e$ (intensity $R$) through a linear filter $G_v$:
$$
\Phi_v(\omega) = G_v(i\omega)\,R\,G_v^*(i\omega), \qquad \text{scalar: } \Phi_v = |G_v(i\omega)|^2R
$$
![[R7026R L6 s19 white vs colored.png]]
Real disturbances are colored: a gyro bias drifts slowly, a solar torque follows the orbit.

> [!important] The disturbance library
> | Disturbance | Shaping filter $G_v(s)$ | Spacecraft example |
> |---|---|---|
> | Constant / slowly drifting | $\frac1s$ (random walk) | solar pressure on a one-sided array, gyro bias |
> | Sinusoid at $\omega_d$ | $\frac{\omega_d}{s^2 + \omega_d^2}$ | torque repeating every orbit, wheel imbalance |
> | Resonance at $\omega_r$ | $\frac{\omega_r^2}{s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_rs + \omega_r^2}$ | flexible solar array, fuel slosh |
> | Band-limited noise | $\frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c}$ | sensor noise |

**A constant disturbance ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=20|s. 20]]):** $G_v = 1/s$ has $|G_v|^2 = 1/\omega^2$, the step's spectrum. Driven by white noise it gives a **random walk** (integrated white noise), whose variance grows: $E\,v^2(t) = t$ (not stationary). Why not an exact constant? A random walk stays near its value for a short time but may wander over a long one, just like a real solar torque or gyro bias. An estimator built on it (L7) **keeps tracking** the value instead of freezing on its first estimate.

**A disturbance at one frequency ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=21|s. 21]]):** $v = \sin(\omega_dt)$ has Laplace transform $\frac{\omega_d}{s^2 + \omega_d^2}$ → that's the filter (an undamped oscillator driven by white noise; like the random walk its amplitude grows without bound, which is fine for an estimator).

**A resonance ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=22|s. 22]]):** random disturbance with a dominating frequency $\omega_r$: the second-order filter of L1 with a small $\zeta$ gives a tall, narrow peak.
![[R7026R L6 s22 resonance.png]]
*Small $\zeta$: a tall, narrow peak at $\omega_r$. The array bends at its own frequency and keeps ringing each time it's shaken ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=22|s. 22]]).*

### Spacecraft example: a torque that repeats every orbit ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=23|s. 23]])
On an Earth-pointing spacecraft the environment looks the same once per orbit, so gravity-gradient, magnetic and solar-pressure torques have a **constant part** plus parts at the **orbital frequency** $\omega_o$ (and harmonics):
$$
T_d(t) \approx T_0 + a\sin(\omega_ot + \varphi)
$$
500 km: $\omega_o = 2\pi/94.6\text{ min} = 1.107\cdot10^{-3}$ rad/s.
![[R7026R L6 s23 orbit periodic torque.png]]
$T_0$, $a$, $\varphi$ **unknown**, $\omega_o$ **known** → a disturbance *model*, not a signal:
- Constant part: $G = \frac1s$: $\dot x_0 = 0$ (+ a little white noise so it may drift).
- Periodic part: $G = \frac{\omega_o}{s^2 + \omega_o^2}$: $\dot x_d = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -\omega_o^2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}x_d$, $T_d = x_0 + [1\ 0]\,x_d$.

Augmented with one axis $I\ddot\theta = T + T_d$: $x = [\theta, \dot\theta, x_0, x_d^T]^T$, **five states**. An observer (L5) or Kalman filter (L7) then **estimates $T_d$**, and the controller can cancel it.

### Space example: reaction-wheel imbalance and jitter ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=24|s. 24]])
![[R7026R L6 s24 wheel imbalance.png]]
A wheel spinning at $\Omega$ with a dynamic imbalance $U_d$ pushes on the spacecraft with a torque that rotates with the wheel:
$$
T_d(t) = U_d\Omega^2\sin(\Omega t + \varphi)
$$
(static imbalance: off-center mass $m$ at radius $r$ gives force $mr\Omega^2$; mass off the mid-plane adds the torque $U_d\Omega^2$.) 3000 rpm: $\Omega = 314$ rad/s = 50 Hz.
- Model: the same harmonic shaping filter at $\Omega$ (known from the tachometer, but changing with wheel speed).
- Far above the attitude-loop bandwidth (~0.1 rad/s): **the controller cannot react to it.**
- Rigid body: $I\ddot\theta = T_d$ ⇒ $\theta(t) = -\frac{U_d}{I}\sin(\Omega t + \varphi)$, a **jitter** of amplitude $U_d/I$.
- If $\Omega$ sweeps through a structural resonance, the jitter is amplified.

The cure is in the hardware (balancing, isolation) or in avoiding certain wheel speeds, but the model tells us where to look.

### Spacecraft example: a gyro noise model ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=25|s. 25]])
A rate gyro measures the body rate plus white noise plus a slowly wandering bias:
$$
\omega_m = \omega + b + v_g, \qquad \dot b = v_b \qquad (v_g, v_b \text{ white})
$$
The bias is integrated white noise (shaping filter $1/s$, a random walk).
![[R7026R L6 s25 gyro bias random walk.png]]
*A simulated gyro at 10 Hz: the noise hides a bias that wanders ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=25|s. 25]]).*

Trick: **use the gyro reading as the input**: $\dot\theta = \omega = \omega_m - b - v_g$. With a star tracker $y = \theta + v_{st}$:
$$
x = \begin{bmatrix} \theta \\ b \end{bmatrix}, \quad
\dot x = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}_A x + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}_B\omega_m + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_N\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} v_g \\ v_b \end{bmatrix}}_{v_1}, \quad
y = \underbrace{[1\ 0]}_C x + \underbrace{v_{st}}_{v_2}
$$
- $v_g$ is **process** noise here (in $v_1$): the gyro reading is the input, so its noise drives the state.
- $\mathcal{O} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$, rank 2: **the bias is observable through the star tracker** (with the gyro alone it isn't, L3).

This is the form $\dot x = Ax + Bu + Nv_1$, $y = Cx + v_2$, the basis of L7.

### Realization: stacking everything into one system ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=26|s. 26–28]])
Three state-space models side by side, each driven by white noise:
1. Plant: $\dot x_a = A_ax_a + B_au + N_aw$, $z = C_zx_a + D_zu$, $y = C_ax_a + Du + n$
2. Process-disturbance filter: $\dot x_w = A_wx_w + B_wv_w$, $w = C_wx_w + D_wv_w$
3. Measurement-noise filter: $\dot x_n = A_nx_n + B_nv_n$, $n = C_nx_n + D_nv_n$

![[R7026R L6 s27 augmented system.png]]
*For a spacecraft: $G_w$ makes the orbit-periodic torque, $G_n$ the colored star-tracker noise, $G_a$ is $I\ddot\theta = u + w$ ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=27|s. 27]]).*

Stack the states $x = [x_a^T\ x_w^T\ x_n^T]^T$:
$$
\dot x = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} A_a & N_aC_w & 0 \\ 0 & A_w & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & A_n \end{bmatrix}}_A x + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} B_a \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}_B u + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} N_aD_w & 0 \\ B_w & 0 \\ 0 & B_n \end{bmatrix}}_N\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} v_w \\ v_n \end{bmatrix}}_{v_1}
$$
$$
z = \underbrace{[C_z\ 0\ 0]}_M x + D_zu, \qquad y = \underbrace{[C_a\ 0\ C_n]}_C x + Du + \underbrace{D_nv_n}_{v_2}
$$
Result: **one LTI system driven only by $u$ and white noise**:
$$
\dot x = Ax + Bu + Nv_1, \qquad z = Mx + D_zu, \qquad y = Cx + Du + v_2
$$
with intensities $R_1$, $R_2$ and cross-intensity $R_{12}$ ($\neq 0$ if $D_n \neq 0$, because both $v_1$ and $v_2$ then contain $v_n$). What we gain: the disturbance states become states the observer/Kalman filter can **estimate**, and the theory only has to handle white noise.

### Spectral factorization ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=30|s. 30–31]])
Flight data give a spectrum $\Phi_v(\omega)$. To augment the model we need the filter: find $G_v$, **stable and minimum-phase**, with $\Phi_v = |G_v(i\omega)|^2$.

Flipping a pole or zero across the imaginary axis doesn't change $|G_v|^2$, so several filters give the same spectrum. Why insist on stable + minimum-phase?
- **Stable:** the filter must produce a stationary signal.
- **Minimum phase:** then $G_v^{-1}$ is stable too, so the filter can be run backwards to turn $v$ back into white noise (the Kalman filter does this with its innovations, L7).

(The $1/s$, sinusoid and gyro-bias filters are *not* stable. As estimator models they're fine if the augmented system stays detectable; for LQ design they're replaced by $1/(s + \delta)$ with a small $\delta > 0$.)

**Recipe:** factor numerator and denominator in $\omega^2$ and **keep the factors with roots in the left half plane**.

(a) $\Phi_u = \frac{a^2}{\omega^2 + a^2} = \frac{a}{i\omega + |a|}\cdot\frac{a}{-i\omega + |a|}$ ⇒ $G(s) = \frac{a}{s + |a|}$.

(b) $\Phi_u = \frac{a^2b^2}{(\omega^2 + a^2)(\omega^2 + b^2)}$ ⇒ $G(s) = \frac{ab}{(s + |a|)(s + |b|)}$.

### Problem: a drag-free spacecraft (GOCE) ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=32|s. 32–35]])
![[R7026R L6 s32 goce drag-free.png]]
GOCE (ESA, 2009–2013) mapped Earth's gravity field from ~255 km, where drag is large and never steady. An ion engine of 1–20 mN cancelled the drag along the track.

Along-track position deviation $z$, thrust $u$, drag $f = k_1\dot z + v$ ($k_1\dot z$: drag grows with velocity deviation; $v$: random density changes), $m\ddot z = u - f$.

**Task 1:** $\ddot z + \frac{k_1}{m}\dot z = \frac1m(u - v)$. State space with $x_1 = z$, $x_2 = \dot z$:
$$
\dot x = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -\frac{k_1}{m} \end{bmatrix}x + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \frac1m \end{bmatrix}u + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -\frac1m \end{bmatrix}v, \qquad z = [1\ 0]\,x
$$
**Task 2, colored drag:** $\Phi_v = \frac{k_0}{\omega^2 + a^2}$ ⇒ $G_v = \frac{\sqrt{k_0}}{s + |a|}$, i.e. $\dot v = -|a|v + \sqrt{k_0}\,e$. Make $v$ the third state:
$$
\dot x = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -\frac{k_1}{m} & -\frac1m \\ 0 & 0 & -|a| \end{bmatrix}x + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \frac1m \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}u + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ \sqrt{k_0} \end{bmatrix}e
$$
The drag model adds one state and a pole at $-|a|$: the slower the density changes, the more low-frequency drag the thruster has to follow.

**Task 3, three kinds of position-sensor noise** ($y = z + n$):

| Sensor noise $n$ | $\Phi_n$ | $G_n(s)$ | extra states | $R_2$ | $R_{12}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (i) white | $0.1$ | $1$ | 0 | 0.1 | 0 |
| (ii) no slow errors (bias calibrated away) | $0.1\frac{\omega^2}{\omega^2 + b^2}$ | $\frac{s}{s + \lvert b\rvert}$ | 1 | 0.1 | $\neq 0$ |
| (iii) slow errors only (drifting sensor) | $\frac{0.1}{\omega^2 + b^2}$ | $\frac{1}{s + \lvert b\rvert}$ | 1 | 0 | 0 |

- (ii): $G_n = 1 - \frac{|b|}{s + |b|}$ has a direct term ($D_n = 1$), so $v_n$ appears in both $v_1$ and $v_2$ → **correlated**, $R_{12} = [0\ 0.1]^T \neq 0$.
- (iii): no white noise left on $y$, $R_2 = 0$. The Kalman gain (L7) contains $R_2^{-1}$ → undefined; the filter would trust $y$ completely. Every real sensor has some white noise: **keep a small $R_2 > 0$**.

→ The **shape** of the noise spectrum decides the **structure** of the estimation problem.

### Discrete time and aliasing ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=37|s. 37–41]])
Every integral becomes a sum. **New:** the spectrum only exists up to $\pi/h$ (half the sampling frequency).
- Size: $\frac{h}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/h}^{\pi/h}\Phi_z\,d\omega = R_z$ (white noise $\Phi \equiv 1$ gives $R_z = 1$).
- Through a system: $\Phi_y = G(e^{i\omega h})\,\Phi_u\,G^*(e^{i\omega h})$.
- Pulse $\delta(k)$ ⇔ white noise, same pairing as in continuous time.

**Sampling a spectrum ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=40|s. 40]]):** the sampled spectrum is
$$
\frac1h\left[\Phi_z(\omega) + \sum_{r\neq0}\Phi_z(\omega + 2r\pi/h)\right]
$$
Everything above $\pi/h$ is **folded back** on top of what we wanted: **aliasing**.

![[R7026R L6 s38 aliasing.png]]
*A wheel at 3000 rpm shakes the spacecraft at 50 Hz; the gyro sampled at 8 Hz can only see up to 4 Hz. The samples show a 2 Hz oscillation that doesn't exist, and the attitude loop will try to fight it ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=38|s. 38]]).*

→ **Filter before sampling** (anti-aliasing filter).

## Formulas
<!-- Only what I must know. Say what each symbol means -->
**Signal size**
$$
\|z\|_2^2 = \int_0^\infty |z|^2dt\ \text{(energy)}, \qquad \|z\|_e^2 = \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\int_0^N|z|^2dt\ \text{(power)}
$$
**Spectrum and size**
$$
\Phi_z(\omega) = \int r_z(\tau)e^{-i\omega\tau}d\tau, \qquad \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty\Phi_z(\omega)\,d\omega = R_z
$$
where $r_z$ = autocorrelation, $R_z$ = covariance (power) or energy matrix.

**Through a system**
$$
\Phi_y = G(i\omega)\Phi_uG^*(i\omega), \qquad \text{scalar: } \Phi_y = |G(i\omega)|^2\Phi_u
$$
**White noise** (intensity $R$)
$$
E\,v = 0, \qquad E\,v(t)v^T(s) = R\,\delta(t - s), \qquad \Phi_v \equiv R
$$
**Impulse energy = white-noise power**
$$
\|g\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int|G(i\omega)|^2d\omega; \qquad \frac{1}{s + a}: \ \frac{1}{2a}; \qquad W\frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c}: \ \sigma^2 = \frac{W\omega_c}{2}
$$
**Shaping filters:** constant $\frac1s$; sinusoid $\frac{\omega_d}{s^2 + \omega_d^2}$; resonance $\frac{\omega_r^2}{s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_rs + \omega_r^2}$.

**Augmented model**
$$
\dot x = Ax + Bu + Nv_1, \qquad y = Cx + Du + v_2, \qquad R_1, R_2, R_{12}
$$
**Discrete:** spectrum only up to $\pi/h$; aliasing folds $\omega + 2r\pi/h$ onto $\omega$.

## Worked example
<!-- AFTER: redo one example from the slides by hand, without looking -->
**Problem:** ([[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=17|s. 17]]) An Earth sensor's noise is white noise of intensity $W = 9\cdot10^{-4}$ deg² s through $G(s) = \frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c}$. Find the rms noise $\sigma$ for $\omega_c = 2$ and $20$ rad/s. Verify the integral in the time domain.

**Solution:**
1. *Output spectrum:*
$$
|G(i\omega)|^2 = \frac{\omega_c^2}{\omega^2 + \omega_c^2}, \qquad \Phi_z = |G|^2W = \frac{W\omega_c^2}{\omega^2 + \omega_c^2}
$$
2. *Variance (frequency domain):* using $\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{d\omega}{\omega^2 + c^2} = \frac{\pi}{c}$:
$$
R_z = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int\frac{W\omega_c^2}{\omega^2 + \omega_c^2}d\omega = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot W\omega_c^2\cdot\frac{\pi}{\omega_c} = \frac{W\omega_c}{2}
$$
3. *Check in the time domain:* impulse response $g(t) = \omega_ce^{-\omega_ct}$,
$$
W\int_0^\infty\omega_c^2e^{-2\omega_ct}dt = W\omega_c^2\cdot\frac{1}{2\omega_c} = \frac{W\omega_c}{2}\ ✓
$$
4. *Numbers:*
   - $\omega_c = 2$: $\sigma^2 = 9\cdot10^{-4}$, $\sigma = 0.030°$
   - $\omega_c = 20$: $\sigma^2 = 9\cdot10^{-3}$, $\sigma = 0.095°$
5. *Interpretation:* 10× wider bandwidth → 10× the variance, $\sqrt{10} \approx 3.2$× the rms. As $\omega_c \to \infty$, $\sigma \to \infty$: pure white noise has infinite power.

## Self-test
<!-- AFTER: 2–3 questions to quiz myself before the exam -->
- Q: Which is a process disturbance and which a measurement disturbance: (a) gyro bias, (b) solar radiation torque, (c) star-tracker noise, (d) fuel slosh?

> [!example]- Answer
> Process (moves the state): (b), (d). Measurement (only corrupts $y$): (a), (c). (But if you use the gyro reading as the *input*, as on [[Lecture_6_handout.pdf#page=25|s. 25]], the gyro noise becomes process noise in that model.)

- Q: White noise of intensity 4 passes through $G(s) = 3$. What is the output spectrum? Is it white?

> [!example]- Answer
> $\Phi_y = |3|^2\cdot4 = 36$, flat → still white, intensity 36. Only a frequency-dependent $G$ colors it.

- Q: What shaping filter would you use for a gyro bias, and why not an exact constant?

> [!example]- Answer
> $G = 1/s$ driven by white noise (a random walk). A real bias drifts slowly; a random walk lets the estimator keep tracking it instead of locking onto its first estimate.

- Q: Find a stable minimum-phase $G$ for $\Phi(\omega) = \frac{9}{\omega^2 + 16}$.

> [!example]- Answer
> $\frac{9}{\omega^2 + 16} = \frac{3}{i\omega + 4}\cdot\frac{3}{-i\omega + 4}$ ⇒ $G(s) = \frac{3}{s + 4}$.

- Q: A 50 Hz vibration is sampled at 8 Hz. What frequency appears, and how do you prevent it?

> [!example]- Answer
> Nyquist = 4 Hz. 50 Hz folds back to $|50 - 6\cdot8| = 2$ Hz. Low-pass filter (anti-aliasing) before sampling.
