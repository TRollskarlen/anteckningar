**Question:** How accurately can a spacecraft point, where does each error source enter the loop and which bandwidth is best, and how do you damp the nutation of a momentum-biased GEO satellite when its actuators are weak and its only sensor is noisy?
**Builds on:** [[R7026R L13]], [[R7026R L6]], [[R7026R L7]], [[R7026R L3]], [[R7026R L4]], [[R7026R L12]] · **Leads to:** [[R7026R L14]]

## Summary
<!-- AFTER: answer the Question in my own words, max 3 sentences -->
Pointing error = bias (calibrate) + slow (integral action or a disturbance model) + random (bandwidth and filtering); in a PD loop sensor noise passes below $\omega_n$ and grows with bandwidth ($\sigma^2 = W_p\omega_n/4\zeta$), torque noise meets the stiffness $I\omega_n^2$ and falls as $\omega_n^{-3}$, so the best bandwidth balances them ($\omega_{n,best}^4 = 3W_w/(I^2W_p)$), independent sources add in variance, and the Lyapunov equation gives the numbers in one line. At GEO, magnetic and solar torques are tiny (~54 µN m), so damping the nutation by differentiating a noisy Earth sensor saturates them, while a quiet loop leaves the nutation undamped. Remedies: LQG (the model predicts the rate), active nutation damping through the strong pitch wheel via a product of inertia and a band-pass network, or two wheels in a V; a constant yaw offset is unobservable from roll and only hardware (more $h$, a yaw sensor) fixes it.

## Key concepts
<!-- One line each: what it is + why it matters -->
- **Pointing requirements:** GEO comsat ~0.05° roll; EO satellite arcseconds + stability < ~1″/s; space telescope milliarcseconds.
- **Bias, jitter, stability:** where the boresight points = constant bias + random jitter; stability = the rate of the jitter.
- **Feedback fights torques, not sensor errors:** sensor bias → calibration; sensor noise → filtering; every loop passes some of each.
- **Three noise paths:** attitude-sensor noise $n_p$, gyro rate noise $n_r$, wheel torque noise $w$; one denominator, three numerators.
- **Error budget:** variance from $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int|G|^2\Phi\,d\omega$; colored noise with corner $\omega_c \gg \omega_n$ ≈ white with $W = 2\sigma^2/\omega_c$; independent sources add in variance.
- **Lyapunov equation:** $AP + PA^T + BWB^T = 0$ gives all variances, no integrals.
- **Best bandwidth:** sensor terms grow with $\omega_n$, torque term falls as $\omega_n^{-3}$; which requirement binds decides.
- **GEO magnetorquers:** 500 A m² → only 54 µN m at GEO (11 mN m in LEO).
- **Nutation network $G_{nut}(s)$:** a band-pass around $\omega_{nut}$ feeding Earth-sensor roll into the pitch wheel command.
- **Product of inertia coupling:** $I_{yz}$ tilts the principal axes by $\delta \approx I_{yz}/(I_z - I_y)$ so the pitch wheel torque reaches yaw.
- **V wheels:** two wheels tilted ±α from the orbit normal: bias $2H\cos\alpha$ plus strong yaw torque.

### How accurately must a spacecraft point? ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=4|s. 4]])
- GEO comsat: beam on the coverage area, ~0.05° in roll.
- EO satellite: arcseconds, and **stability** below ~1″/s during each image or the picture smears.
- Space telescope: milliarcseconds over many minutes.

![[R7026R E2 s4 bias jitter and stability.png]]
*Where the boresight points: a constant **bias** plus a random **jitter**; the **stability** is the rate of the jitter ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=4|s. 4]]).*

Question 1: feedback fights **torques**; it can't correct a sensor that is itself wrong. Sensor bias → calibration; sensor noise → filtering; every loop passes some of each.

### Where the errors enter the loop ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=5|s. 5–6]])
One axis, PD on a wheel torque, three noise sources (each white noise through a first-order filter):
- attitude-sensor noise $n_p$ (Earth sensor ~0.03°, star tracker a few ″),
- gyro rate noise $n_r$ (angle random walk),
- wheel torque noise $w$ (friction ripple, imbalance, current noise).

![[R7026R E2 s5 PD loop with three noise sources.png]]
*The PD loop with its three noise sources (Sidi Fig. 7.3.10, [[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=5|s. 5]]).*

With $K = I\omega_n^2$, $K_d = 2\zeta\omega_nI$:
$$
\theta(s) = \frac{-\omega_n^2n_p(s) - 2\zeta\omega_nn_r(s) + w(s)/I}{s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_ns + \omega_n^2}
$$
**One denominator, three numerators.**
![[R7026R E2 s6 each noise has its own path.png]]
*Left: sensor noise passes straight to the attitude below $\omega_n$; a faster loop lets more through. Right: torque noise meets the stiffness $I\omega_n^2$; a faster loop resists it better ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=6|s. 6]]).*

**A conflict: no single bandwidth is best for both; the error budget decides.**

### From the spectrum to the variance ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=7|s. 7]])
L6: $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int|G(i\omega)|^2\Phi(\omega)\,d\omega$. For white noise of intensity $W$ (backup [[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=33|s. 33]] uses $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\frac{b_0^2}{|D|^2} = \frac{b_0^2}{4\zeta\omega_n^3}$ and $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\frac{b_1^2\omega^2}{|D|^2} = \frac{b_1^2}{4\zeta\omega_n}$):

| noise source | pointing $\sigma_\theta^2$ | stability $\sigma_{\dot\theta}^2$ |
|---|---|---|
| attitude sensor $W_p$ | $\dfrac{W_p\omega_n}{4\zeta}$ | $\dfrac{W_p\omega_n^3}{4\zeta}$ |
| gyro $W_r$ | $\dfrac{W_r\zeta}{\omega_n}$ | $W_r\zeta\omega_n$ |
| wheel torque $W_w$ | $\dfrac{W_w}{4\zeta I^2\omega_n^3}$ | $\dfrac{W_w}{4\zeta I^2\omega_n}$ |

- Colored noise with rms $\sigma$ and corner $\omega_c \gg \omega_n$ ≈ white with $W = 2\sigma^2/\omega_c$ (L6).
- Independent sources add in **variance**: $\sigma_{tot}^2 = \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 + \sigma_3^2$.
- Read the powers of $\omega_n$: sensor terms grow with bandwidth, the torque term falls as $\omega_n^{-3}$.

### Worked example: an EO satellite ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=8|s. 8–10]])
$I = 600$ kg m², $\omega_n = 0.15$, $\zeta = 0.7$; star tracker 10″ rms (10 rad/s), gyro ARW 0.005°/√h, wheel torque noise 5 mN m rms (100 rad/s). Requirement (3σ): pointing 36″, stability 1″/s.

| rms | pointing | stability |
|---|---|---|
| star tracker | 1.03″ | 0.15″/s |
| gyro | 0.65″ | 0.10″/s |
| wheel torque | **2.50″** | **0.38″/s** |
| total | 2.78″ | 0.42″/s |
| 3σ | 8.3″ ✓ | 1.25″/s ✗ |

The wheels dominate both. Isolators that halve the torque noise → 0.26″/s rms (0.78″/s 3σ): met. (All numbers re-derived in the worked example below.)

**Check ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=9|s. 9]]):** state $x = [\theta\ \dot\theta\ n_p\ w]^T$ with the noise filters as extra states, $\dot x = Ax + Be$:
$$
AP + PA^T + BWB^T = 0, \qquad \sigma_\theta^2 = P_{11}, \quad \sigma_{\dot\theta}^2 = P_{22} \qquad (\texttt{P = lyap(A, B*W*B')})
$$
![[R7026R E2 s9 monte carlo vs lyapunov.png]]
*One hour of simulated noise: 2.80″ rms vs 2.78″ from the Lyapunov equation ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=9|s. 9]]).*

No integrals, no white-noise approximation: one line of MATLAB for any loop.

**The bandwidth trade-off ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=10|s. 10]]):** $\sigma_\theta^2 = \frac{W_p\omega_n}{4\zeta} + \frac{W_w}{4\zeta I^2\omega_n^3}$; set the derivative to zero:
$$
\omega_{n,best}^4 = \frac{3W_w}{I^2W_p}
$$
![[R7026R E2 s10 pointing error vs bandwidth.png]]
*Pointing error vs loop bandwidth: star tracker rises, wheel torque falls; the total has its minimum at 0.31 rad/s (1.77″ rms) vs the design 0.15 ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=10|s. 10]]).*

Question 2: raise to 0.31? Not for imaging: stability gets worse (0.53″/s), and flexible solar arrays limit the bandwidth anyway. **The best bandwidth depends on which requirement binds.**

### Second example: a GEO comsat ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=11|s. 11]])
Pitch with a momentum wheel, $I_y = 500$, no gyro: PD ($\zeta = 0.7$) differentiates the Earth-sensor angle (0.03° rms, 2 rad/s). Antenna allows 0.05° (3σ).
![[R7026R E2 s11 GEO pitch with earth sensor.png]]

| $\omega_n$ [rad/s] | $\sigma_\theta$ | $3\sigma_\theta$ |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.0095° | 0.029° ✓ |
| 0.3 | 0.0157° | 0.047° (just) |
| 1.0 | 0.0246° | 0.074° ✗ |

Without a gyro the sensor noise also passes the derivative: $\theta/n_p = -\frac{2\zeta\omega_ns + \omega_n^2}{s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_ns + \omega_n^2}$ ⇒ $\sigma_\theta^2 \approx W_p\omega_n\left(\zeta + \frac{1}{4\zeta}\right)$. Here sensor noise dominates and disturbances are tiny: **the lower the bandwidth, the better**. GEO loops run around 0.05–0.1 rad/s.

### Bias, slow and random errors ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=12|s. 12–13]])
![[R7026R E2 s12 bias slow and random errors.png]]
- **Bias** (misalignment, thermal distortion): calibration, not feedback.
- **Slow** (constant or once-per-orbit torque): PD leaves $\theta_{ss} = T_d/(I\omega_n^2)$; integral action or a disturbance model removes it (L6, L7). Example: 20 µN m → 0.31″, small against the random 2.8″.
- **Random:** bandwidth and filtering.

A real budget lists every source in one of the three columns, adds the random ones in variance, and adds the biases on top.

**Gyros alone ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=13|s. 13]]):** during slews or eclipses: noise integrates to a random walk $\sigma_\theta = \text{ARW}\sqrt t$ (0.005°/√h → 0.005° after 1 h, 0.016° after 10 h); an uncorrected bias of 0.01°/h adds 0.1° in 10 h (linear, soon dominates).
![[R7026R E2 s13 gyro error growth.png]]
*Integrated-gyro error grows without limit; each star-tracker update resets it ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=13|s. 13]]).* → gyros fly with a star tracker and a Kalman filter that also estimates the bias (L7, L10).

### Roll–yaw control with magnetic torques at GEO ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=15|s. 15–20]])
L13's roll-only law ($T_{cx} = -(k_x\phi + k_{xd}\dot\phi)$, $T_{cz} = aT_{cx}$) realized with two magnetorquers. At GEO $\mathbf B \approx B_y\mathbf j$ (orbit normal), so $T_{cx} = -M_zB_y$, $T_{cz} = M_xB_y$: **$M_z$ steers roll, $M_x$ steers yaw**. No fuel, no wear. But they can't unload the pitch wheel (its momentum lies along $\mathbf B$) → thrusters.
![[R7026R E2 s15 magnetorquers at GEO.png]]

**How strong? ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=16|s. 16]])**
![[R7026R E2 s16 field strength vs altitude.png]]
*The dipole field falls as $1/r^3$: 22 800 nT at 700 km, 108 nT at GEO. A 500 A m² magnetorquer gives 11 mN m in LEO but only 54 µN m at GEO ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=16|s. 16]]).*

Question 3: enough for a 5 µN m disturbance? Yes. For the sensor noise the loop turns into torque commands: next.

**Sensor noise at the magnetorquers ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=17|s. 17]]):** L13's satellite ($h = 20$, $k_x = 0.00427$, $a = -0.888$, filters at 0.2 rad/s), Earth-sensor noise 0.03° rms:

| $k_{xd}$ | nutation $\zeta$ | $T_{cx}$ rms |
|---|---|---|
| 42.8 | 0.52 | 1.0 mN m (19× the limit) |
| 4.28 | 0.083 | 92 µN m (1.7×) |
| 0.428 | 0.0043 | 9.1 µN m |
| 0.214 | −0.0004 | unstable |

![[R7026R E2 s17 differentiated sensor noise.png]]
*$k_{xd}s$ differentiates the noise before it reaches the ±54 µN m magnetorquer ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=17|s. 17]]).*

**Damping the nutation needs the rate, and the rate comes from differentiating a noisy angle: good damping saturates the magnetorquers.**

- **Nominal gains in Simulink ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=18|s. 18]]):** torque at its limit 95 % of the time (bang-bang); roll error 0.016° rms instead of the linear 0.0065°. A saturated actuator is no longer the designed linear loop.
  ![[R7026R E2 s18 saturated magnetorquer torque.png]]
- **Low gain $k_{xd} = 0.428$ ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=19|s. 19]]):** ~10 µN m rms, never saturated, but $\zeta = 0.0043$: the nutation (period 280 s) decays with a 3-hour time constant; halve the gain and it grows.
  ![[R7026R E2 s19 undamped nutation.png]]

Question 4: quiet magnetorquers *and* damping? Yes: a better rate estimate (LQG) or a stronger actuator that does only the damping (the pitch wheel).

**Other cases ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=20|s. 20]]):** at 700 km the field is 200× stronger, so the nominal 1 mN m fits a 100–500 A m² rod (2–11 mN m): no saturation. **Solar torques** at GEO (tilting the panels, "solar sailing") give only 10–20 µN m: same problem, worse. Real case: ESA's OTS showed a nutation instability in orbit, driven by dissipation in its momentum wheel. **GEO is the hard case: weak actuators, undamped nutation, 15 years to survive.**
![[R7026R E2 s20 solar sailing.png]]
*North and South panels tilted in opposite senses make a small windmill torque from radiation pressure ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=20|s. 20]]).*

### LQG on roll and yaw ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=21|s. 21–22]])
Yaw is observable from roll (rank 4, L3/L13). A Kalman filter on the Earth-sensor roll estimates $\dot\phi$, $\psi$, $\dot\psi$ **from the model**, with no differentiation. LQR with Bryson weights (0.05° roll, 0.4° yaw, 0.1 mN m per rod).
![[R7026R E2 s21 LQG loop.png]]

| | nutation $\zeta$ | noise torque rms | steady roll / yaw ($T_d$ = 5 µN m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PD, $k_{xd} = 0.428$ | 0.0043 | 9.1 µN m | 0.05° / 0.33° |
| LQG | 0.17 | 3.5 µN m | 0.006° / 0.41° |

**40× the damping, less noise torque, much smaller roll error**: the model predicts the rate instead of differentiating noise. Yaw stays near its 0.4° allowance (seen only through roll). Design details: backup [[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=35|s. 35]].

**Can the filter remove the yaw offset? (Question 5, [[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=22|s. 22]])** Augment the filter with constant disturbances (L6, L7): with $T_{dx}$ rank 5 of 5 (observable); with $T_{dz}$ rank **4 of 5: not observable**. A constant yaw torque gives a constant yaw $\psi = T_{dz}/(\omega_oh)$, and a constant yaw leaves **no trace in roll**.
![[R7026R E2 s22 yaw invisible to earth sensor.png]]
*Yaw turns the Earth sensor about the nadir line: the Earth disc stays centered ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=22|s. 22]]).*

**No estimator can remove an unobservable error: only a larger bias or a yaw sensor reduces it.**

### Damping the nutation with the pitch wheel ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=24|s. 24–28]])
The pitch wheel is strong (~0.1 N m) but acts about $Y_B$ only. A **product of inertia** $I_{yz}$ couples pitch and yaw:
$$
\begin{bmatrix} I_y & -I_{yz} \\ -I_{yz} & I_z \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \ddot\theta \\ \ddot\psi \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} u_w \\ T_z \end{bmatrix} + \dots \ \Rightarrow\ \ddot\psi \approx \frac{I_{yz}}{I_yI_z}u_w
$$
(Invert the 2×2: off-diagonal of the inverse is $I_{yz}/(I_yI_z - I_{yz}^2) \approx I_{yz}/(I_yI_z)$.)
![[R7026R E2 s24 product of inertia tilt.png]]
*Seen along $X_B$: an off-axis mass (offset reflector) tilts the principal axes by $\delta \approx I_{yz}/(I_z - I_y)$; the wheel torque about $Y_B$ then has a component about the principal yaw axis ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=24|s. 24]]).*

$I_{yz} = 20$ kg m² on $I_y = 500$: a tilt of only 2.3°, enough to borrow the wheel's torque for yaw. (Backup [[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=34|s. 34]]: at GEO the $\omega_o$ terms times $I_{yz}$ are negligible at the nutation frequency.)

**The control scheme ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=25|s. 25]]):** take Earth-sensor roll, keep only the part near the nutation frequency, add it to the pitch-wheel command:
$$
u_w = u_{pitch} + G_{nut}(s)\,\phi_m
$$
The magnetic loop stays quiet with $k_{xd} = 0.214$ (alone unstable!). **Two actuators, two jobs**: magnetorquers hold roll/yaw slowly, the wheel damps the fast nutation.
![[R7026R E2 s25 nutation network loop.png]]
*Roll loop with $T_{cz} = aT_{cx}$, pitch loop, and the nutation network $G_{nut}(s)$ from roll to pitch torque (Sidi Fig. 8.5.1, [[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=25|s. 25]]).*

**The nutation network ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=26|s. 26]]):**
$$
G_{nut}(s) = \frac{K_ns}{(1 + s/0.03)(1 + s/0.02)}
$$
- The $s$ in the numerator: a constant roll error must not drive pitch ($|G_{nut}| = 0.15$ N m/rad at $\omega_o$).
- The two poles pass only frequencies near $\omega_{nut} = h/\sqrt{I_xI_z} = 0.022$ rad/s ($|G_{nut}| = 24$ N m/rad there).
- Above 0.03 rad/s the gain falls as $1/\omega$: little sensor noise passes. But $\omega_{nut}$ shifts 10–15 % as $h$ and the inertias change.

![[R7026R E2 s26 nutation band-pass.png]]
*$K_n = 2000$: a band-pass around the nutation frequency (shaded: ±15 %) ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=26|s. 26]]).*

L6's resonant shaping filter used the other way round: act only where the problem lives.

**Result ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=27|s. 27]]):** $k_{xd} = 0.214$, $I_{yz} = 20$:

| $K_n$ | nutation $\zeta$ | pitch rms |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | −0.0004 | unstable |
| 2000 | 0.055 | 0.022° |
| 4000 | 0.12 | 0.041° |

![[R7026R E2 s27 nutation damped by pitch wheel.png]]
*With $G_{nut}$ the nutation decays with a ~800 s time constant (three nutation periods) instead of 3 h ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=27|s. 27]]).*

Magnetorquers ~5 µN m rms, a tenth of their limit. **The sign of $K_n$ matters** ($-2000$ → $\zeta = -0.049$). Price: roll noise now reaches pitch; $K_n = 2000$ keeps pitch at 0.022° rms, inside the 0.05° budget.

**Robustness ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=28|s. 28]]):** from $h = 17$ to 23 N m s, $\zeta$ stays 0.052–0.055 ($K_n = 2000$) and 0.11–0.12 ($K_n = 4000$).
![[R7026R E2 s28 damping vs momentum bias.png]]
Question 6: why not a passive damper (L12)? At a 280 s period it's large and weak, and must outweigh the wheel's destabilizing term (L12's dual-spin condition with wheel = rotor, $\omega_{nut} = \lambda_P$). The active network reuses the wheel and sensor already on board.

### Two momentum wheels in a V ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=29|s. 29]])
Two wheels in the $Y_B$–$Z_B$ plane, tilted ±α from the orbit normal:
$$
h_{wy} = -(H_1 + H_2)\cos\alpha, \quad h_{wz} = (H_1 - H_2)\sin\alpha \ \Rightarrow\ T_{cy} = (\dot H_1 + \dot H_2)\cos\alpha, \quad T_{cz} = -(\dot H_1 - \dot H_2)\sin\alpha
$$
![[R7026R E2 s29 two wheels in a V.png]]
*(a) Two wheels in a V and a backup wheel $MW_z$; (b) the total momentum; (c) after a failure $MW_z$ restores the direction (Sidi Fig. 8.7.1, [[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=29|s. 29]]).*

- Together: bias $h = 2H\cos\alpha$ along the orbit normal **and** a strong yaw torque (no saturation by noise).
- Noise in $T_{cz}$ is amplified by $1/\sin\alpha$ at the wheels: α ≈ 25° is a reasonable compromise.
- Example: 20 N m s bias with α = 25° → two wheels of 11.0 N m s; a 10 µN m yaw torque needs $\dot H_1 - \dot H_2 = 23.7$ µN m (×2.37).
- The V also lets the antenna be re-pointed in roll and pitch on command while yaw stays stiff.

## Formulas
<!-- Only what I must know. Say what each symbol means -->
**PD loop with noise:** $\theta = \frac{-\omega_n^2n_p - 2\zeta\omega_nn_r + w/I}{s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_ns + \omega_n^2}$.

**Variances (white noise):** sensor $\frac{W_p\omega_n}{4\zeta}$, gyro $\frac{W_r\zeta}{\omega_n}$, torque $\frac{W_w}{4\zeta I^2\omega_n^3}$; rates: $\frac{W_p\omega_n^3}{4\zeta}$, $W_r\zeta\omega_n$, $\frac{W_w}{4\zeta I^2\omega_n}$.

**Colored → white:** $W = 2\sigma^2/\omega_c$. **Add in variance.** **Lyapunov:** $AP + PA^T + BWB^T = 0$.

**Best bandwidth:** $\omega_{n,best}^4 = \frac{3W_w}{I^2W_p}$. **No gyro (PD on Earth sensor):** $\sigma_\theta^2 \approx W_p\omega_n(\zeta + \frac{1}{4\zeta})$.

**Slow error:** $\theta_{ss} = T_d/(I\omega_n^2)$. **Gyro only:** $\sigma_\theta = \text{ARW}\sqrt t$, bias error $= bt$.

**GEO magnetorquer:** $T_{max} = MB$, $B_{GEO} \approx 0.11$ µT.

**Pitch–yaw via $I_{yz}$:** $\ddot\psi \approx \frac{I_{yz}}{I_yI_z}u_w$, $\delta \approx \frac{I_{yz}}{I_z - I_y}$. **Nutation network:** $G_{nut} = \frac{K_ns}{(1 + s/0.03)(1 + s/0.02)}$.

**V wheels:** $h = 2H\cos\alpha$, $T_{cz} = -(\dot H_1 - \dot H_2)\sin\alpha$.

## Common mistakes
- **Adding rms values.** Independent random errors add in *variance*: $\sigma_{tot} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 + \dots}$. Biases are added on top, linearly.
- **1σ against a 3σ requirement.** Multiply the rms by 3 before you compare (the EO stability fails only at 3σ).
- **Mixed units in one formula.** The torque term has $I$ in it and comes out in rad². Convert the sensor intensity to rad² s before you compare or combine it with the torque term (step 6 of the worked example).
- **Colored → white too early.** $W = 2\sigma^2/\omega_c$ only holds when the corner $\omega_c$ is well above the loop bandwidth $\omega_n$.
- **ARW per root hour.** 0.005°/√h = 0.3″/√s: divide by $\sqrt{3600} = 60$ when going to seconds.
- **Raising the bandwidth to fix pointing.** It makes stability (the rate error) worse, and flexible solar arrays limit it anyway. Check which requirement binds.
- **Hoping an estimator removes a constant yaw offset.** It is unobservable from roll. Only a larger $h$ or a yaw sensor helps.
- **The sign of $K_n$.** With $-2000$ the nutation network *destabilizes* ($\zeta = -0.049$). Check the sign in a root locus or a simulation.

## Worked example
<!-- AFTER: redo one example from the slides by hand, without looking -->
**Problem:** ([[Lecture_E2_handout.pdf#page=8|s. 8, 10]]) Re-derive the EO error budget: $I = 600$ kg m², $\omega_n = 0.15$ rad/s, $\zeta = 0.7$; star tracker 10″ rms with corner 10 rad/s; gyro ARW 0.005°/√h; wheel torque noise 5 mN m rms with corner 100 rad/s. (a) Pointing and stability rms of each source and the total. (b) Is 1″/s (3σ) met? (c) The bandwidth that minimizes pointing error.

**Solution:**
1. *Intensities:* star tracker $W_p = 2\cdot10^2/10 = 20$ arcsec² s. Gyro: 0.005°/√h $= 0.005\cdot3600''/60\sqrt{\text{s}} = 0.3''/\sqrt{\text{s}}$ → $W_r = 0.09$ (″/s)² s. Wheel: $W_w = 2(5\cdot10^{-3})^2/100 = 5\cdot10^{-7}$ N² m² s. Also $4\zeta = 2.8$.
2. *Star tracker:* $\sigma_\theta^2 = 20\cdot0.15/2.8 = 1.07$ → 1.03″; $\sigma_{\dot\theta}^2 = 20\cdot0.15^3/2.8 = 0.024$ → 0.15″/s.
3. *Gyro:* $\sigma_\theta^2 = 0.09\cdot0.7/0.15 = 0.42$ → 0.65″; $\sigma_{\dot\theta}^2 = 0.09\cdot0.7\cdot0.15 = 0.0095$ → 0.10″/s.
4. *Wheel torque:* $4\zeta I^2 = 2.8\cdot3.6\cdot10^5 = 1.008\cdot10^6$.
   - $\sigma_\theta^2 = \frac{5\cdot10^{-7}}{1.008\cdot10^6\cdot0.003375} = 1.47\cdot10^{-10}$ rad² → $1.21\cdot10^{-5}$ rad = 2.50″
   - $\sigma_{\dot\theta}^2 = \frac{5\cdot10^{-7}}{1.008\cdot10^6\cdot0.15} = 3.31\cdot10^{-12}$ → $1.82\cdot10^{-6}$ rad/s = 0.38″/s
5. *Totals (add variances):* pointing $\sqrt{1.07 + 0.42 + 6.25} = 2.78''$ (3σ 8.3″ < 36″ ✓). Stability $\sqrt{0.024 + 0.0095 + 0.141} = 0.42''$/s → 3σ 1.25″/s **> 1″/s ✗**. Halving the torque noise (variance /4: 0.035) → $\sqrt{0.0686} = 0.26''$/s, 3σ 0.78″/s ✓.
6. *Best bandwidth:* in radians $W_p = 20\cdot(4.848\cdot10^{-6})^2 = 4.70\cdot10^{-10}$ rad² s:
$$
\omega_{n,best}^4 = \frac{3\cdot5\cdot10^{-7}}{(600)^2\cdot4.70\cdot10^{-10}} = \frac{1.5\cdot10^{-6}}{1.69\cdot10^{-4}} = 8.9\cdot10^{-3} \ \Rightarrow\ \omega_{n,best} = 0.31\ \text{rad/s}
$$
   But stability gets worse there, so for imaging keep 0.15 and fix the wheel noise.

## Self-test
<!-- AFTER: 2–3 questions to quiz myself before the exam -->
- Q: Which kind of pointing error can feedback remove, and which needs something else?

> [!example]- Answer
> Feedback removes the effect of torques (disturbances, slow errors with integral action). Sensor bias needs calibration; sensor noise needs filtering and the right bandwidth; every loop passes some sensor noise to the attitude.

- Q: Why does sensor noise grow with bandwidth while torque noise shrinks?

> [!example]- Answer
> Sensor noise enters with the numerator $\omega_n^2$ like the command, so it passes straight through below $\omega_n$; a wider loop passes more ($\sigma^2 \propto \omega_n$). Torque noise meets the loop stiffness $I\omega_n^2$: a stiffer loop resists it ($\sigma^2 \propto \omega_n^{-3}$).

- Q: A GEO comsat's PD on the Earth sensor gives 0.074° (3σ) at $\omega_n = 1$ rad/s. What do you change?

> [!example]- Answer
> Lower the bandwidth: sensor noise dominates and disturbances are tiny. At 0.1 rad/s it's 0.029° (3σ) ✓.

- Q: Why does good nutation damping saturate GEO magnetorquers, and what are three remedies?

> [!example]- Answer
> Damping needs $\dot\phi$, obtained by differentiating a noisy Earth-sensor angle; with $k_{xd} = 42.8$ the noise torque is 1 mN m vs a 54 µN m limit. Remedies: LQG (the model predicts the rate), damping with the pitch wheel through $I_{yz}$ and a nutation band-pass, or two wheels in a V.

- Q: Can a Kalman filter on roll remove a constant yaw offset caused by a yaw disturbance torque?

> [!example]- Answer
> No. Augmented with $T_{dz}$ the system is rank 4 of 5: a constant yaw angle $\psi = T_{dz}/(\omega_oh)$ leaves no trace in roll. Only a larger $h$ or a yaw sensor reduces it.

- Q: Two V wheels at α = 30° must give a 20 N m s bias. Wheel momentum? Yaw torque amplification?

> [!example]- Answer
> $H = h/(2\cos\alpha) = 20/(2\cdot0.866) = 11.5$ N m s each. $\dot H_1 - \dot H_2 = T_{cz}/\sin\alpha = 2T_{cz}$: factor 2.
